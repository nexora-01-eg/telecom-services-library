# ROLLZ: THE HERO WHO CLOCKED OUT

## Format

A five-episode comic-video story told through **40 cinematic images**: eight images per episode. The intended master format is **vertical 9:16**, designed for short-form video. Each image should remain on screen for approximately 3–5 seconds, with subtle camera movement, sound design, and English dialogue captions added during editing.

## Core Premise

In the neon city of **Metrovale**, the city’s most powerful protector, **Captain Crave**, is exhausted. After years of stopping villains, catching falling trains, and answering emergency calls at impossible hours, he makes one simple wish: to take a night off, sit inside his favorite restaurant, and eat the sandwich he loves most.

But the night he chooses to retire, a mysterious signal begins draining the city’s energy. The signal leads back to **Rollz**, a warm, comic-book-themed sandwich restaurant with yellow-and-black colors, golden hanging bulbs, wooden tables, and walls covered in illustrated hero panels. Captain Crave must decide whether rest means abandoning responsibility—or finding a smarter, more human way to be a hero.

## Visual Bible

The world combines **cinematic superhero action, warm comic-book illustration, and cozy restaurant realism**. Rollz is the emotional home of the story: a welcoming neighborhood restaurant with bold yellow branding, black accents, warm amber lighting, wooden tables, graphic-novel wall art, and a visible sign reading **ROLLZ**. The restaurant should feel original and brand-specific, not like a recognizable DC or Marvel location. Any wall comics must use invented characters and symbols.

Captain Crave is a tall, athletic man in his late 30s with expressive tired eyes, short dark hair, and a small silver streak at one temple. His superhero suit is midnight navy with warm yellow panels, a rolled-up cape, and a stylized circular “C” emblem. Off duty, he wears a charcoal hoodie, yellow sneakers, and a red scarf. His signature power is **gravity control**, represented by soft golden rings and floating dust—not lightning.

**Mina**, the owner of Rollz, is a quick-witted woman in her early 30s with curly black hair, round glasses, a yellow apron, and a black shirt. She is calm under pressure and knows every customer’s comfort order. **Byte**, a tiny hovering repair drone shaped like a toasted bread cube, speaks in short beeps and projects blue holographic symbols. The antagonist is **The Deadline**, a faceless shadow made from clocks, red notification lights, and torn calendar pages. It feeds on exhaustion and turns every urgent task into a crisis.

The emotional tone is adventurous, funny, warm, and suspenseful. The central message is: **rest is not weakness; it is part of sustainable courage.**

# EPISODE 1 — THE LAST SHIFT

## Image 01 — “Another Night in Metrovale”

Wide establishing shot of Metrovale at night, a futuristic city with elevated trains, glowing yellow street signs, and rain reflecting neon on the streets. Captain Crave flies between buildings, visibly exhausted, his golden gravity rings flickering. Caption: **“For ten years, Captain Crave had saved the city every night.”**

## Image 02 — “One More Emergency”

Captain Crave catches a runaway city bus with gravity control while commuters cheer below. His smile is forced; his shoulders sag. A tiny red alert glows on his wrist. Dialogue: **“You’re safe. I’m… totally fine.”**

## Image 03 — “The Impossible To-Do List”

Close-up of his wrist display showing an absurd list: rescue, patrol, repair, investigate, repeat. The final line reads: **“EAT DINNER.”** It is crossed out three times. A shadow of a clock stretches behind him.

## Image 04 — “The Decision”

On a quiet rooftop, Captain Crave removes his helmet and looks at the sunrise beginning behind the skyline. He holds a folded Rollz takeaway menu with a yellow-and-black design. Dialogue: **“Tonight, I clock out.”**

## Image 05 — “Rollz After Midnight”

Exterior of Rollz at night. The bold **ROLLZ** sign glows above a comic-book mural, warm hanging bulbs visible through the windows, wooden tables inside. Rain falls softly. A handwritten window sign says: **“OPEN LATE.”**

## Image 06 — “The Favorite Seat”

Captain Crave enters Rollz in civilian clothes: charcoal hoodie, yellow sneakers, red scarf. Mina recognizes him but does not make a fuss. She points to a corner table beneath a painted fictional hero mural. Dialogue: **“Your table survived without you.”**

## Image 07 — “The Perfect Roll”

Mina presents his favorite sandwich, the **Golden Hero Roll**: toasted bread, smoky grilled filling, crisp lettuce, bright sauce, wrapped in custom yellow paper. Steam rises like a tiny cape. Captain Crave looks genuinely happy for the first time.

## Image 08 — “The Signal”

Just as Captain Crave takes the first bite, every light in Metrovale flickers. The Rollz sign buzzes. A dark clock-shaped symbol appears in the air outside. Byte rises from behind the counter and projects a warning. Caption: **“His night off lasted exactly four seconds.”**

# EPISODE 2 — THE CITY THAT WOULD NOT SLEEP

## Image 09 — “Power Drop”

The restaurant falls into partial darkness. Only the warm bulbs over the wooden tables remain lit. Outside, trains stop and skyscrapers lose their lights in a wave moving toward Rollz.

## Image 10 — “The Deadline Arrives”

The Deadline appears across the street: a towering silhouette made of clocks, calendar scraps, and red notification lights. Its face is blank except for one glowing countdown. Dialogue: **“REST IS A DELAY.”**

## Image 11 — “The First Attack”

Shadowy clock-creatures pour through the street, chasing tired citizens carrying phones and briefcases. Captain Crave instinctively steps outside, but Mina grabs his sleeve. Dialogue: **“You said you were off duty.”**

## Image 12 — “A Different Kind of Hero”

Captain Crave looks back at his untouched sandwich, then at the frightened people outside. He puts on only one yellow glove, leaving his hoodie on. Dialogue: **“I’m not going back to the old way.”**

## Image 13 — “Gravity, Gently”

He uses soft golden gravity rings to slow the clock-creatures instead of smashing them. Their hands stop spinning. The action feels precise and controlled, not destructive.

## Image 14 — “Mina’s Counterattack”

Mina activates Rollz’s hidden emergency system: the comic wall panels slide open, revealing a compact map room built behind the mural. Yellow lines connect Rollz to power stations throughout Metrovale. Dialogue: **“Every good neighborhood has a backup plan.”**

## Image 15 — “Byte Finds the Pattern”

Byte projects a holographic map. The Deadline’s signal is not attacking the city randomly; it is following delivery routes and restaurant power lines. A pulsing mark appears beneath Rollz.

## Image 16 — “Beneath the Restaurant”

A secret staircase opens beneath the counter. Captain Crave, Mina, and Byte descend into a glowing underground tunnel while the warm restaurant lights fade above them. Caption: **“The answer was under his favorite table.”**

# EPISODE 3 — THE ENGINE UNDER ROLLZ

## Image 17 — “The Hidden Kitchen”

The tunnel opens into an impossible underground chamber: old subway tracks, glowing yellow cables, delivery lockers, and a giant circular machine shaped like a sandwich press. The machine hums with stolen city energy.

## Image 18 — “The Original Promise”

A faded mural shows the founders of Rollz helping exhausted workers during Metrovale’s first blackout. Mina explains that the restaurant was built around a simple promise: **“Feed the city. Keep it moving. Let people breathe.”**

## Image 19 — “The Stolen Minutes”

Glass jars filled with glowing clock-hands hang from the ceiling. Each jar is labeled with an emotion: sleep, laughter, quiet, family, dinner. Captain Crave sees one labeled **“HEROES.”**

## Image 20 — “The Truth About The Deadline”

The Deadline emerges from the machine and reveals that it was created from every emergency alert, unpaid hour, and impossible expectation in the city. Dialogue: **“You built me every time you said, ‘I can handle one more thing.’”**

## Image 21 — “Captain Crave Breaks”

Close-up of Captain Crave’s face as he admits the truth. The golden rings around his hands collapse into sparks. Dialogue: **“I don’t know who I am when I’m not saving everyone.”**

## Image 22 — “Mina’s Answer”

Mina hands him half of the Golden Hero Roll and sits beside him on an old wooden crate. Dialogue: **“Then start by saving one person. Start with yourself.”**

## Image 23 — “The Plan”

The team studies the machine. Byte identifies three control points: the clock core, the signal tower, and a pressure valve that can release the stolen energy. Mina draws the plan on a paper sandwich wrapper.

## Image 24 — “Rollz Goes Underground”

A dramatic action image: Mina straps on a utility belt, Byte projects a route, and Captain Crave ties his red scarf like a makeshift cape. Behind them, the machine powers up and the Deadline’s countdown drops to **00:10:00**.

# EPISODE 4 — THE TEN-MINUTE WAR

## Image 25 — “The Clock Core”

Captain Crave enters the machine chamber alone while enormous gears rotate around him. He uses gravity to create a path across floating metal platforms. Golden rings illuminate the darkness.

## Image 26 — “Mina vs. The Signal Tower”

Mina races through a corridor of flickering yellow cables toward the signal tower, carrying a Rollz sauce bottle modified as a bright energy tool. Byte flies beside her, projecting arrows.

## Image 27 — “The City’s Panic”

Above ground, Metrovale citizens stare at frozen clocks and endless notifications. The Deadline appears on every screen. The city feels trapped in a loop of urgency.

## Image 28 — “The Old Instinct”

The Deadline launches a massive wave of red clock-light at Captain Crave. He starts to absorb it, trying to carry the whole burden himself. His suit begins to crack with light.

## Image 29 — “The New Choice”

Captain Crave stops absorbing the energy and instead sends a golden gravity pulse through the floor. The pulse divides the burden into thousands of small glowing sparks that travel toward the city.

## Image 30 — “Everyone Helps”

Across Metrovale, citizens work together: a mechanic restarts a train, children hold open a shelter door, a nurse shares a quiet meal, and delivery riders guide people home. The city is no longer waiting for one hero.

## Image 31 — “The Sauce Button”

Mina reaches the signal tower and presses a glowing button labeled **“EXTRA SAUCE.”** The modified bottle releases a golden wave that changes the red notification lights into warm yellow lanterns.

## Image 32 — “The Deadline Falls Silent”

The Deadline shrinks from a towering shadow into a small cracked alarm clock. Captain Crave catches it gently. Its final display reads: **“NO EMERGENCY.”**

# EPISODE 5 — CLOCKED OUT

## Image 33 — “The Quiet After”

The underground chamber is calm. The stolen clock-hands return to their jars, then float upward toward the city. The machine transforms into a glowing circular mural.

## Image 34 — “The City Breathes”

Metrovale at dawn. Trains move, lights glow, and people walk more slowly. For the first time, Captain Crave watches the city without hovering above it.

## Image 35 — “A New Sign”

Back at Rollz, Mina replaces the old emergency notice with a hand-painted sign: **“HEROES NEED DINNER TOO.”** The warm bulbs shine against the comic-book wall.

## Image 36 — “The Final Sandwich”

Captain Crave sits at his favorite wooden table in his hoodie, not his armor. The Golden Hero Roll is in front of him. Byte rests beside the plate like a tiny pet. He takes a slow, satisfied bite.

## Image 37 — “A Table for More”

Other heroes and ordinary workers begin entering Rollz: a firefighter, a bus driver, a street cleaner, a student, and a masked neighborhood guardian. Everyone sits together at wooden tables beneath the fictional comic mural.

## Image 38 — “The New Rule”

Captain Crave hangs his helmet on a hook near the door. A small plaque beside it reads: **“OFF DUTY IS A SUPERPOWER.”** He smiles, relaxed and present.

## Image 39 — “One Last Alert”

A tiny alert appears on Byte’s screen: **“UNKNOWN THREAT: HUNGER.”** Everyone looks at Captain Crave. He raises his sandwich protectively. Dialogue: **“This emergency can wait five minutes.”**

## Image 40 — “Rollz After Dark”

Final hero shot of Rollz at night, glowing yellow and black against Metrovale’s skyline. Through the window, Captain Crave laughs with Mina and the community. The **ROLLZ** sign is clear and prominent. Final caption: **“Some heroes save the city. The best ones remember to live in it.”**

# Production Notes for the Five Videos

Each episode should run approximately **35–55 seconds**. Use slow push-ins for emotional restaurant moments, quick parallax movement for action images, gentle handheld motion inside Rollz, and stronger zooms during appearances of The Deadline. The soundtrack should begin with tense electronic pulses, then gradually blend in warm percussion and a playful rhythm associated with a busy sandwich shop.

Use English captions exactly as written in the dialogue and caption lines. Keep the Rollz sign, yellow-and-black palette, warm bulbs, wooden tables, and comic mural consistent in every restaurant shot. For image generation, create the character reference sheet first and use it as the visual anchor for all 40 frames. Generate the frames without long dialogue text inside the image; add precise English captions during video editing for reliable readability.

# Master Image Prompt

Create a vertical 9:16 cinematic comic-book illustration for the original superhero story “Rollz: The Hero Who Clocked Out.” Preserve strict character consistency: Captain Crave, tall athletic tired-looking man in his late 30s, short dark hair with one silver streak, midnight navy superhero suit with warm yellow panels and a circular C emblem, or charcoal hoodie with yellow sneakers and red scarf when off duty; Mina, curly black hair, round glasses, yellow apron over a black shirt; Byte, a tiny hovering toasted-bread-shaped repair drone. Set the scene in the original city of Metrovale or inside Rollz, a warm sandwich restaurant with a bold yellow-and-black ROLLZ sign, amber hanging bulbs, wooden tables, and invented comic-book wall art. Use cinematic lighting, expressive faces, dynamic but readable composition, polished graphic-novel style, rich textures, consistent proportions, and no recognizable copyrighted superhero characters. Avoid distorted hands, extra limbs, random logos, illegible long text, real-world comic trademarks, and visual style drift.
